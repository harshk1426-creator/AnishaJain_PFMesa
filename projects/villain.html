01 — The Core Insight

Men buy fragrance by occasion, not by notes

The target consumer — young men aged 18–30 — want affordable fragrances that smell good, last long and feel premium. They are not fragrance experts and don't choose by olfactory profile.

They choose based on where they are going and the impression they want to make — a fresh daytime look, a work environment, a late-night party, or a premium evening occasion. This occasion-led approach simplifies the buying journey and naturally encourages owning multiple fragrances.

One collection, four settings

Midnight Villain — built for the night: nightlife, parties and after-hours. Dark, seductive, emotionally intense.

Klaus — built for making impressions: dates, dinners and weddings. Refined, quiet-luxury masculinity.

Default — built for every day: casual, daytime, travel. Clean, versatile, effortlessly wearable.

Powerplay — built to perform: office, college and the gym. Sharp, energetic and confidence-driven.

03 — Go-to-Market

A sampling-led launch that removes risk

Rather than launching cold, 10–20ml sample vials are inserted into existing Villain orders — leveraging an already-acquired customer base at near-zero acquisition cost.

Each vial carries a QR feedback card — a simple "Like it or Not?" — capturing real data on likability and purchase intent, while building a warm retargeting audience for launch day.

The launch sequences across seven channels — Amazon and Flipkart first for scale, then website bundles, then Myntra and Tira for premium positioning, with quick-commerce last.

Priced and measured for profitable scale

The pricing strategy holds a ₹599 MRP with channel-specific selling prices, defending premium perception on Tira and Myntra while competing hard on Flipkart.

The marketing budget is deliberately performance-first — 50% to Amazon — with units sold as the north-star KPI, targeting 8–12k units per month within the first 3–4 months.
